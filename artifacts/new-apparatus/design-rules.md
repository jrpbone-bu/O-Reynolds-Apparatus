## 1. Orientation and layout

- Define front as the view shown in Revision K. Left and right mean image-left and image-right. Up is vertical; back is away from the front observer.
- Arrange the lower assembly from left to right: collection tank 08, dry pump bay containing pump 09, reservoir 10.
- Place the tall main chamber 04 directly above collection tank 08. Place upper vessel 02 and support 03 above chamber 04 on the tower axis.
- Use one connected structural frame 11. Preserve Revision K's shortened right-hand footprint; do not restore the longer reservoir or frame from earlier revisions.
- Do not infer front-to-back dimensions from this front elevation. Establish depth so components fit, remain supported and can be serviced without collisions.

## 2. Structural support — component 11

- Provide continuous connected lower base rails beneath both tanks and the pump bay, and a connected horizontal rail at the tops of the lower tanks.
- Support tank bottoms over their required bearing areas. Rails alone are not proof that a glass bottom has adequate support; specify suitable decking, pads or other support after tank construction is selected.
- Tower members carry the main chamber, media, liquid load, upper support and upper vessel down to the base. Do not use glass seams, plumbing or hoses as structural columns.
- Keep the pump bay dry and physically distinct from both tank interiors. Seat the pump's mounting feet on a supported platform at the lower frame base; do not suspend it from the pipework.
- Do not restore the extra exposed upright between the pump and reservoir removed in earlier revisions. Provide necessary tank support through the base, end frame and suitably located depth members without obstructing the intake connection.
- Route pipe penetrations beside structural members or through deliberately designed clearances. A pipe must not occupy the same solid volume as a beam or post; overlap in a front projection does not authorize a 3D collision.

## 3. Upper vessel and main chamber — components 02, 03 and 04

- Keep vessel 02 centered over the tower and supported by the top plate/frame 03. Preserve the open spacer region between the main chamber top and this upper support.
- Components 02 and 03 form one grouped, detachable upper assembly. Include the glass plate at the top boundary of chamber 04 in this assembly; it holds the thin central tube in position and also acts as chamber 04's cap/enclosure.
- Model the glass cap and its tube penetration as physical parts with a supported seating interface and appropriate sealing detail. Keep the tube centered and restrained without imposing bending loads on the glass. Allow vertical removal clearance for the detachable assembly and its attached tube/needle parts; provide releasable interfaces wherever removal requires disconnection.
- Chamber 04 is a tall rectangular transparent enclosure supported at its bottom and restrained by the surrounding frame. Retain its height and elevation relationship shown in Revision K.
- Keep the slender central tube vertical on the chamber axis. It must remain substantially thinner than the blue pipes.
- Terminate the central tube at the media region with one plain, smooth, constant-diameter nonmetal cylindrical tube. Do not replace this end with a stepped metal fitting, flange or stack of reducers.
- The needle is an existing hidden component of the thin-tube assembly within chamber 04, not an optional future addition. In operating orientation it points downward and is housed inside the white nonmetal cylinder at the bottom of the thin tube. Keep the cylinder's exterior plain; do not replace it with an exposed needle or metal fitting.
- The hidden needle relays water from chamber 04 to the outlet pipe above meter 07. Provide a continuous internal fluid connection between these parts. The precise passages, seals and detachable interfaces must be resolved without inventing a bypass or an additional external pipe.
- Keep this needle native to the chamber 04 assembly; do not duplicate it in vessel 02, the pump or either lower tank. Represent it as a separate hidden part for cutaway/exploded inspection, while keeping it concealed inside the white cylinder in the normal assembled view.
- Retain the rounded media shown at the chamber bottom. Its composition, purpose and exact packing are unspecified.
- Do not add a needle projecting above vessel 02. The inverted reference photograph shows the detached assembly upside down; the installed needle remains downward inside chamber 04's white cylinder.
- Do not invent a fluid connection between vessel 02 and the central tube merely because their axes coincide. Confirm that interface before assigning fluid behavior.

## 4. Chamber ports and external manifold — components 05 and 06

- Provide exactly four side connections through the chamber's right-hand wall, ordered from highest to lowest as P1, P2, P3 and P4.
- Each internal pipe stub points horizontally left into the chamber. Its axis must be exactly collinear with its corresponding external horizontal pipe, with no vertical displacement, refraction offset or internal elbow.
- Show the four internal stubs at the same elevations as the four outside pipes. Use the external pipe axes as the alignment reference. Keep their open ends clear of the central tube and chamber walls.
- P1 is the highest plain branch. P2 and P3 are the two valved branches. These three branches connect to one common vertical blue header on the right.
- Mount both branch valve controls at the rear of their valve bodies, as depicted in Revision K. Their visible handle orientation is upright; do not move their operating axes onto the top of the pipe as in superseded drawings. Provide rear access and full operating clearance.
- Place the common header over the interior footprint of reservoir 10. Its lower end descends through the open top into the reservoir, as shown, without intersecting the frame or glass.
- P4 is the lowest branch and has no valve. It turns downward into an independent pump delivery riser.
- Do not connect P4's riser to the common header. Do not add a lower cross-connection between the two vertical blue runs.
- Use compatible pipe fittings at elbows, tees, reducers and connections. Independently support long pipe runs so their weight and operating loads do not bear on glass penetrations.
- Use sealed bulkhead-type interfaces or an equivalent engineered penetration detail at tank/chamber walls. Provide actual clearance and sealing geometry; do not simply intersect a pipe with a glass surface.

## 5. Chamber outlet and meter — component 07

- Maintain the water path: chamber 04 → hidden needle inside the white cylinder → connected chamber outlet → straight upper pipe above meter 07 → inline meter → brass handwheel valve → straight lower pipe → collection tank 08. This describes connectivity, not an assumed unverified passage shape inside the cylinder.
- Keep the upper and lower straight pipe sections visible. The lower section below the valve is approximately half the upper section's exposed length, following the adopted design intent. Exact lengths remain to be established.
- Orient the red valve handwheel toward the left end of the apparatus, matching the collection tank drain's physical direction. Keep it reachable and clear of the tower.
- Route the discharge through a planned shelf/frame clearance into tank 08. Keep fittings accessible for removal; do not bury the meter or valve in a structural beam.
- Meter axis, installation direction, straight-run requirements and suitability must be checked against the selected meter's requirements. The illustration does not establish measurement accuracy.

## 6. Collection tank — component 08

- Fill the clear lower bay between the tower pillars with one rectangular collection tank. Do not narrow it to accommodate the pump and do not place the pump inside it.
- Receive the chamber discharge from component 07 above.
- Tank 08 is closed at the top and fixed in place. Its top enclosure is at the marked boundary below component 07; model an actual fixed cover, not an open water surface or a detachable lid like the chamber cap.
- Pass the discharge pipe through a properly sealed opening in this fixed top enclosure. Retain the left-side drain and keep all tank penetrations physically distinct from the supporting frame.
- A closed cover does not establish a pressure-vessel rating or hermetic sealing. Resolve air displacement during filling/draining and any required vent connection before fabrication; do not assume a sealed air pocket can accept continuous inflow or add an unapproved visible vent to the reference model.
- Place the low drain on the tank's left side, projecting left, with its control accessible. Locate it close to the tank bottom while allowing adequate wall edge distance and fitting clearance.
- There is no direct pump or hose connection to tank 08.

## 7. Pump and reservoir — components 09 and 10

- Seat pump 09 on the lower base in the dry bay to the right of tank 08 and left of reservoir 10. Keep the pump body clear of both tanks and their support members.
- Connect the reservoir's low left-side port to the pump through a short horizontal green reinforced hose. This is the only tank connected directly to the pump.
- Connect the pump's upper discharge to the independent P4 riser. Use a straight vertical green reinforced hose immediately above the pump, transitioning to blue rigid pipe above it.
- Preserve Revision K's increased blue pipe and green hose lengths below the upper base rail. The green section is a real flexible connector with suitable end connections, not a support pillar.
- Keep the pump inlet hose, reservoir port and pump inlet aligned without forced bending or mechanical preload. Verify inlet/outlet identities against the chosen pump before implementation.
- Reservoir 10 remains rectangular, to the right of the pump, and accepts the common header's descending end within its interior.
- Both lower tanks have the deeper downward-extended geometry adopted in Revision K. Their top rims share the upper rail elevation; their bottoms are supported at the lower base level.
- Keep the reservoir intake port low enough for the intended pump supply while preserving wall strength, sealing space and usable clearance from the tank bottom.

## 8. Connection logic

The intended arrangement established by the user's corrections is:

1. Reservoir 10 → short horizontal green hose → pump 09.
2. Pump 09 → vertical green connector → blue delivery riser → lowest unvalved chamber port P4.
3. Chamber ports P1, P2 and P3 → common blue header → reservoir 10. P2 and P3 each contain a valve.
4. Chamber 04 → hidden needle housed in the white cylinder → connected outlet pipe above meter 07 → meter 07 → handwheel valve → fixed closed collection tank 08 → separate left-side drain.

These are connection rules, not a prediction of actual flow rates or branch flow directions under every operating condition. Do not introduce an automatic return from tank 08 to reservoir 10 or assume the collection path forms a closed loop. The needle's water-relay function is user-confirmed; the detailed internal passage geometry and the media's purpose remain unspecified.

## 9. Materials and visual modeling

- Use black structural members, transparent enclosure panels, blue rigid plumbing, green reinforced flexible connectors, orange branch/drain controls and a red meter-valve handwheel.
- Represent chamber/tank surfaces with transparent cyan or green-tinted edges without visually refracting pipe positions.
- Model separate parts and real joints for tanks, frame, fittings, valves, meter, pump and hoses. Do not merge glass with pipes or treat overlapping silhouettes as physical connections.
- The exact transparent panel material, pipe grade, sizes, seal materials and metal finishes remain specification decisions. Color is not proof of material or pressure rating.

## 10. Change and dimensional rules

- Revision K controls over all previous visual iterations. Future changes must be explicit and recorded as a new revision.
- The user-confirmed hidden construction and closure details recorded above supplement Revision K without replacing its external layout. Earlier notes treating the needle as future-only or leaving tank 08 open are superseded.
- Preserve adopted placements and connections while resolving missing dimensions. Do not invent dimensions from pixels or claim the image is to scale.
- Changes to lower hose/pipe lengths must keep the pump seated on the base and reconcile tank depth, frame depth and port elevation together.
- Changes above the meter must reconcile the chamber support elevation, tower uprights and external riser lengths while preserving branch alignment.
- Specify overall dimensions, depth, frame sections, tank panel thickness, support details, pipe diameters, fitting envelopes, service clearances and component specifications before fabrication.
- Check stability, supported loads, glass penetrations, pump conditions, drainage and meter installation once sizes and operating conditions are known. Revision K alone does not establish those capacities.

## 11. Acceptance checks for the 3D model

- Front projection follows Revision K's arrangement and relative proportions.
- Four internal blue stubs align exactly with four exterior pipe axes and face left.
- Only P2 and P3 have branch valves; their controls are rear-mounted.
- P4 remains independent of the upper-three-port common header.
- Common-header discharge lies inside reservoir 10's footprint.
- Pump connects directly to reservoir 10 and P4 only, never tank 08.
- Pump feet rest on the base; blue/green pump delivery sections remain straight and distinct.
- Tank 08 fills its tower bay; both lower tanks retain the adopted deeper geometry.
- Components 02 and 03, including the tube-supporting glass cap of chamber 04, form a detachable upper assembly with sufficient removal clearance.
- The downward needle is concealed inside chamber 04's white cylinder and connects hydraulically to the pipe above meter 07.
- Tank 08 has a fixed closed top with a sealed discharge-pipe penetration; do not apply this fixed closure rule to the detachable upper cap or infer a closure change for reservoir 10.
- Meter/valve and the straight pipe sections above and below remain accessible and unobstructed.
- No pipe/frame/glass solid collisions, unsupported tank bottoms, or hoses used as structural members.
- No upward-projecting needle or superseded extra reservoir-side upright.
